    Radius is specificity

    Every dot is one of 5,595 categories in a 2-dimensional Poincaré disk. Broad categories sit near the centre; the most specific leaves press against the rim. Red is the path from the root to your selection.
    Purple are geodesics to its siblings — the shortest routes between two categories that sit almost on top of each other at the rim. They plunge inward, passing shallower than the parent. In this space the quickest way between two specific things is through the general. That is why hierarchies fit here and not in flat space.
    Radius along that shortest path. It dips below the amber line — below the siblings' own parent. On the disk these arcs overlap into one stripe, which is why the profile is plotted instead.
    Euclidean HNSW alone finds only a fraction of the true hyperbolic neighbours. The Formula Query rescore recovers them.
    Watch the r column. Hyperbolic search walks inward — it returns the ancestors, at smaller radius (green). The flat and text panels return siblings sitting at roughly the same radius: semantically close, hierarchically useless.

    The model was never shown a depth label

    It only ever saw is-an-ancestor-of links. So the radius it learned is what the structure implies about how general a category is. Where that disagrees with the level the category is actually filed at, the taxonomy is often the thing that is wrong. Residual below is a robust z-score computed within each depth, so it means "strange for its level", not merely "deep".
    –Spearman(radius, depth)
    –mean residual, leaf nodes
    –mean residual, hub nodes
    –Spearman(residual, #children)
    Each dot is a category: depth across, learned hyperbolic radius up. The bands are tight — radius really does encode level. The red dots are the categories whose radius fights their level.
