Point a camera at a medicine box. Get the same molecule, in another country.

Polish, Spanish and Dutch national registries joined on ATC code and never on brand name — Apap and ANTIDOL are the same drug and share no characters. This page is the measurement record behind that demo: what was tried, what shipped, and the two results that came back against expectation.

SHIFT: one subtraction instead of a retrained model

Estimate a single offset vector per language as the mean gap to the pivot, then subtract it: indexed = embed(doc) − α · offset[lang]. Nothing is retrained. Spanish is the pivot, so it has no offset by construction. The measure that matters is cross-border hits — how many results come back in the language you are actually shopping in.

Recall barely moves across the whole sweep; the offset is not finding new documents, it is reordering which language surfaces. α=0.75 is the measured peak for both source languages and is what the demo ships. The two languages were aligned to Spanish independently — there is no Polish↔Dutch offset anywhere, yet both land in the same corrected space.

This reads like a typo and is not. Arctic's cross-border hits at 256 dimensions are higher than at 1024. The tail dimensions carry language-specific detail that SHIFT is trying to remove anyway, so dropping them helps slightly. The control is what makes that claim safe rather than anecdotal: same cut, no MRL objective, and it falls to 86%. Four retrievers, same 300 queries

Polish queries against Spanish documents, which is what the cross-border lookup actually does. Fusion is reciprocal rank fusion, used because BM25 scores and cosines are not on a shared scale.

Half of this went as predicted and half did not. BM25 fails the way the theory says it must: on 160 of 300 queries it shares no term at all with any Spanish document, so the result is empty rather than badly ranked. That is the vocabulary gap, and it is the entire argument for dense retrieval here. But SPLADE does not fail — term expansion reaches across that gap, and fusing it with dense beats dense alone at every cut. That was not the expected result, and it holds despite Splade_PP being English-trained on Spanish and Polish text. The shipped path is still dense + hyperbolic, because +0.010 at top-1 does not pay for a second index and a 500 MB model — a cost decision, not an accuracy one.

Hyperbolic space fits a drug classification tree

ATC is a five-level hierarchy. Hyperbolic space has exponentially more room as radius grows, which is the shape a tree needs; Euclidean space does not and has to crush the levels together. Both embeddings below have the same dimensionality and the same training budget.

Poincaré disk. Radius encodes specificity: broad categories near the centre, individual substances at the rim. Mean hyperbolic radius runs 4.29 → 8.40 from level 1 to level 5, cleanly ordered. Plotted at hyperbolic distance from the origin, not raw coordinates — in raw coordinates all five levels sit between 0.92 and 0.9994 of the boundary and the structure is invisible. That crowding is the metric working, not a flaw.

Euclidean. Same tree, same budget, plotted at its own norm — which is already its metric, so no transform is applied. The levels interleave instead of separating: there is not enough room out at the edge to keep them apart.

This is not decoration. The 10-dimensional Poincaré embedding is what reranks the live results, and it fixes a specific error cosine cannot: for a paracetamol box it ranks ANTIDOL DUAL (paracetamol plus ibuprofen, N02BE51) above plain ANTIDOL (N02BE01). The strings are near-identical so no embedding separates them, but they are different codes, and handing someone a combination product when they asked for one molecule is a real error. Graded tree distance degrades in the order a pharmacist walks a shelf: same molecule, same chemical subgroup, same therapeutic class.

What was rejected, and why that matters

Three things were built, measured, and not shipped. They are listed because a pipeline is defined as much by what it leaves out.

Late interaction (ColBERT)

0.972

vs 0.984 for dense+hyperbolic on real packaging OCR. MaxSim should tolerate noisy queries by letting junk tokens take their best match — but carton junk is other Spanish pharmaceutical text, which matches confidently in the wrong direction. Pooling dilutes it, so averaging is the feature here, not the flaw.

BM25 / lexical

0.230

160 of 300 queries share no term with any target document. Not a tuning problem — an empty result set. Caveat: fastembed has no Polish stemmer, which understates it.

Embedding-based language ID

0.947

The elegant option — score unshifted, since that is the space where languages have not yet merged. A plain token-frequency vote gets 0.987 on the same carton photos, and every blend of the two was worse than the count alone.

Reading the country off the box

A language picker is a lie about the product — nobody holding a carton first tells the search engine what country they are standing in. It is also a live hazard: SHIFT applies a per-language offset, so the wrong choice does not soften the result, it applies a Dutch-shaped correction to a Polish vector.

Held-out registry text

0.999

Token-frequency vote, 1,200 held-out strings across three languages.

Real carton OCR

0.987

150 packaging photos through Apple Vision — the exact string the camera path produces.

With abstain rule

1.000

Confidence = the winning vote's lead over the runner-up. At 0.2 it catches every error in both tests while flagging 0.4% of queries.

Abstaining falls back to the pivot — the one language with no offset — so uncertainty degrades to plain unshifted retrieval rather than to a confident correction in the wrong direction. The threshold was fitted, not chosen: every observed error sat below it. NUROFEN alone correctly reads as uncertain, because it is genuinely sold in both Poland and the Netherlands and there is nothing in those seven characters to separate them. Add one word — Nurofen voor — and it commits. The carton figure is Spanish-only: every cached photo comes from the Spanish registry, so this measures the false-positive rate on one language and leaves the other two unmeasured.
